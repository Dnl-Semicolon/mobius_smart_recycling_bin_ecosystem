

| PROJECT TITLE:	SMART WASTE BIN ECOSYSTEM FOR BEVERAGE STORE RECYCLING WITH AI-BASED SORTING AND MONITORING |  |  |  |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
|  |  |  |  |
| **ABSTRACT** Improper waste disposal continues to pose environmental challenges, especially in beverage stores where materials like plastic lids, paper cups, and straws are often mixed and discarded incorrectly. Many recycling solutions remain non-interactive or require manual sorting, leading to contamination and low recovery rates. Mobius addresses this gap through a smart waste bin ecosystem designed specifically for beverage stores. The system combines artificial intelligence (AI), computer vision, and real-time data tracking to automate and improve recycling behavior. Each bin is powered by a Raspberry Pi and uses a YOLOv11 model to classify waste items through live image recognition, sorting them into the correct compartments automatically. A QR-based identification system links each disposal to an individual user profile, enabling personalized feedback and gamified rewards through a mobile app. Store owners can access web dashboards to view analytics, manage campaigns, and monitor disposal behavior. By connecting AI-driven automation with user engagement, Mobius promotes responsible recycling habits in commercial environments. Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, Smart Waste Bin, Beverage Store, Smart Recycling, Computer Vision |  |  |  |

| **PROBLEM** |  |  |  |
|  | The exponential rise of single-use beverage packaging has become a growing environmental concern. This issue is especially noticeable in busy commercial areas filled with drink-focused retailers like bubble tea shops, cafés, and specialty beverage chains. In Malaysia, demand for beverages has grown steadily in recent years, driven by changing consumer lifestyles and the rising popularity of convenient and specialty drink options ([Statista, 2025](#statista.-\(2025\).-beverages---malaysia.-statista.-https://www-statista-com.tarc.idm.oclc.org/outlook/io/manufacturing/consumer-goods/beverages/malaysia)). As beverage sales continue to rise, the use of single-use packaging such as plastic cups, straws, and paper sleeves, has increased significantly. This has led to large volumes of packaging waste being generated on a daily basis, raising growing concerns about sustainability and environmental impact ([Statista, 2025](#statista.-\(2025\).-beverages---malaysia.-statista.-https://www-statista-com.tarc.idm.oclc.org/outlook/io/manufacturing/consumer-goods/beverages/malaysia)). A 2022 waste composition study by the Solid Waste Management and Public Cleansing Corporation (SWCorp) found that plastic materials made up about 21.86% of municipal solid waste across 26 landfill sites in selected states of Peninsular Malaysia ([SWCorp, 2022](#solid-waste-and-public-cleansing-management-corporation.-\(2022\).-annual-report-2022.-swcorp-malaysia.-https://www.swcorp.gov.my/en/annual-reports-2022/)). Despite rising awareness about recycling and sustainability, the actual disposal process still falls short, as noted during preliminary field observations of several beverage retailers. Most drink-focused retailers do not provide clear guidance or proper bins to help customers separate recyclables from general waste. Because of that, a lot of potentially recoverable material ends up contaminated and unusable. Field research conducted at CHAGEE, a beverage outlet located at Karpal Singh Drive, Penang, exemplifies this issue. A total of four trash bins are available within the store: two in front-of-house (12L and 25L) and two in the back-of-house (25L each). All waste, regardless of material, is thrown together by customers and staff alike, with no attempts made to separate plastic lids, paper cups, or straws. As confirmed by the branch manager, Muhammad Afiq Afnan (interviewed on 26 July 2025), the outlet does not provide signage or visual cues for sorting. Waste collection is carried out multiple times a day, typically once at 4:00 PM and again during store closing at 10:00 PM. However, this regular bin emptying is not linked to any formal collection service. Instead, waste is placed behind the store in a shared corridor space and is picked up by independent collectors on a first-come, first-served basis. This informal practice means that there is no certainty regarding when, how, or by whom the waste is collected, nor is there any tracking of recycling rates, disposal volumes, or environmental impact. This lack of separation is not solely a procedural flaw. It also reflects a deeper behavioral issue among customers. Recycling, in many public environments, is a passive act rather than a conscious habit. Most customers throw everything into a single bin by default when there are no visible instructions, feedback, or cues. There is no system in place to educate or reinforce proper recycling behavior, nor are there incentives to encourage it. In contrast, gamified mobile systems such as Shopee Farm or health apps have shown that people are responsive to visual progress, feedback loops, and rewards ([Bitrián et al., 2021](#bitrián,-p.,-buil,-i.,-&-catalán,-s.-\(2021\).-enhancing-user-engagement:-the-role-of-gamification-in-mobile-apps.-journal-of-business-research,-132,-170–185.-https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2021.04.028)). Without similar engagement mechanisms at the point of disposal, beverage store customers remain unaware of the impact of their actions. The failure to gamify or personalize the recycling experience contributes to the ongoing cycle of contamination. As a result, even the most advanced recycling infrastructure becomes less effective. The absence of structured waste pickup agreements and the passive role of stores in managing their waste stream reveals a significant gap between consumption and sustainability accountability. In this system, beverage outlets have no control over how their waste is handled once it leaves their premises. Although managers are aware that the recyclables collected can be monetized by third-party collectors, stores like CHAGEE do not participate in this value chain. According to Afiq, the manager of the CHAGEE branch, the store has no intention of profiting from recyclables and allows collectors to take them freely, without any form of negotiation or expectation. Consequently, these outlets miss the opportunity to monetize recyclables, gather useful analytics, or integrate environmentally responsible practices into their operations. Furthermore, the absence of data transparency and traceability is a missed opportunity for businesses to align with Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) goals. Today, companies across industries are starting to recognize the value of sustainability data. They see it not only as an ethical responsibility, but also as a way to meet reporting standards and gain a competitive edge in the market ([SAP, 2024](#sap.-\(2024,-august-14\).-what-is-esg-reporting?-https://www.sap.com/resources/esg-reporting-guide)). Yet many beverage retailers still operate without knowing how much waste they produce, how often it’s collected, or how much of it could actually be recycled. Without a system to track and make sense of this information, it becomes much harder to make informed decisions, take meaningful action, or demonstrate accountability. Another significant issue concerns the logistical inefficiencies in the current waste collection system. Because there is no formal pickup schedule and collections happen arbitrarily, stores cannot plan around waste overflow, and collectors cannot optimize their routes. This lack of coordination results in missed opportunities to reduce travel time, fuel consumption, and labor costs for collectors, particularly when operating at scale across multiple outlets. Interview data shows that CHAGEE’s weekday sales average 330–380 cups per day, rising to 700 on Fridays and up to 1000 on weekends and public holidays. Such high daily output places constant pressure on bin capacity, often requiring staff to monitor and respond to waste levels manually, without support from any coordinated collection system. Ultimately, the current state of beverage outlet waste disposal in Malaysia reflects an unstructured, inefficient, and environmentally disconnected system. Outlets lack both the motivation and the tools to participate in responsible waste management, while waste collectors operate in informal, undocumented cycles that limit scale, accountability, and efficiency. This disconnect across the ecosystem not only undermines recycling efforts, but also prevents the development of a circular waste economy that could benefit all stakeholders. The waste problem will continue to grow alongside the beverage industry unless three things change: the waste disposal process needs to be digitized, vendors need access to meaningful data, and recycling must be supported through clear, structured incentives.  |  |  |

| **Solution** |  |  |  |
|  | To address the fragmented and inefficient nature of beverage-related waste disposal, the solution will be implemented as an integrated ecosystem branded as Mobius. This ecosystem is composed of four main platforms: the smart bin, a mobile application for public-facing users, a web application for administrators and beverage store owners, and a dedicated mobile application for waste collection agents. Each platform plays a distinct role within the ecosystem and contributes toward a seamless end-to-end waste management experience tailored specifically for beverage outlets and their related stakeholders. Artificial intelligence will be embedded at two critical points in this ecosystem: a fine-tuned object detection model will power the smart bin’s ability to classify and sort incoming waste items, while a dedicated route optimization engine will compute the most efficient collection paths based on bin status, location clustering, and cutoff-based scheduling. These AI features allow the system to sort waste accurately based on what users throw in, and to plan efficient collection routes using real-time data from the bins. The smart bin will function as the system’s physical intake point, where user interaction and waste classification begin. This device is responsible for accepting and sorting materials, alongside initiating a data trail that links physical waste to digital records. The bin will be equipped to detect various waste item types, such as plastic lids, coated paper cups, and drinking straws, and to sort them into appropriate internal compartments. Each bin will continuously track its fill level and transmit this information to the central system before a predefined daily cutoff time, which will be set at 7:00 AM. This data forms the basis for route generation, allowing the AI engine to schedule next-day pickups only for bins that have crossed fullness thresholds and were flagged in time. By anchoring route planning to these submitted statuses, the system ensures that collection resources are dispatched efficiently, with no unnecessary visits to underutilized bins. In doing so, the bin serves both as a self-contained sorting mechanism and a sensor node within the broader network of disposal points. In addition to scheduled pickups, the system will also support limited dynamic route updates. If a store marks their bin for collection after the 7:00 AM cutoff, and the assigned collection agent has not yet left their current area or begun traveling to the next, the system will evaluate whether the bin is in the upcoming area cluster. If so, the bin will be added to the route, the path will be re-optimized, and notifications will be sent to both the store and the collection driver. If the driver has already begun transitioning to the next area, the request will be deferred and the store will be informed that their bin will be included in the next day’s pickup cycle. This approach ensures faster service when possible without disrupting existing route plans, and provides transparency to all parties involved. To facilitate user engagement, a mobile application will be developed using the Flutter framework. This app will support Android, iOS, and iPad operating systems and serve as the primary interface for the general public. The application will generate a personal QR identifier displayed on the user’s phone. This QR code will be scanned by a reader on the smart bin before each disposal, linking the physical act of waste separation to the user’s profile. This will enable the system to record each disposal session, award points, and track personal recycling behavior over time. Users will be able to view their disposal history, monitor their cumulative sustainability impact, and earn virtual trees as progress-based rewards. These trees will serve as visual milestones, growing and evolving as users continue to recycle across multiple sessions. The application will allow users to set personal recycling goals. These goals may include the number of cups recycled or the number of active disposal days per week. Users can then track their progress toward unlocking specific tree types or reaching higher reward tiers. Each tree represents a distinct stage in the user’s recycling journey, and new designs will become available as higher milestones are achieved. Using the app will not be required in order to use the bin. Users who choose not to scan their QR code may still dispose of waste as usual, and the system will continue to sort the materials and log the disposal data anonymously. However, their actions will not be attributed to a specific profile, and they will not receive points or rewards. This approach ensures that the bin remains universally accessible, while offering additional value to users who opt into the digital experience. Beverage outlets participating in the ecosystem will be able to subscribe to limited-time engagement boosts. These boosts will allow stores to activate features such as double point rewards, store-wide visibility on the app’s home screen, and access to branded reward campaigns. For example, a newly launched outlet can use the boost to appear prominently within the app and attract foot traffic by offering double points for each disposal made at their location. Stores may also sponsor exclusive virtual trees tied to their brand identity, which users can only unlock by recycling at that specific outlet during the campaign period. This subscription-based model provides businesses with a flexible way to drive customer engagement, promote sustainability efforts, and reinforce brand presence through both functional rewards and digital collectibles. Complementing the user-facing app is a browser-accessible web application, developed using Laravel. This platform will serve administrative, analytical, and operational purposes, catering to multiple roles including beverage store owners, system administrators, and waste collection agencies. Through this interface, authorized users can view real-time data on disposal events, assess bin activity across different locations, assign or evaluate collection tasks, and generate reports for environmental, operational, or corporate sustainability use cases. Store owners will be given tools to review their branch’s waste performance, engage with gamification data from their customer base, and optionally select or rate their preferred collection agents. These proposed features would allow businesses to convert recycling participation into brand value. To support the operations of waste collection agencies, a dedicated mobile application will be developed specifically for registered collection agents. This app will serve as their primary tool for navigating daily pickup assignments. Once signed in, collectors will receive an optimized route generated by the system’s AI engine, which considers bin fullness, area groupings, and the 7:00 AM cutoff time. The application will include a fully interactive map interface, allowing agents to view their assigned bins, track which locations have been completed, and receive real-time updates if dynamic additions are made to their route. Each pickup task will be marked with clear information such as the bin's material type, fill status, and location. This ensures that collectors can plan their time efficiently while avoiding backtracking or unnecessary travel. The entire system will be built on a coordinated stack of technologies that serve different roles across the ecosystem. The mobile applications for both the public-facing users and the collection agents will be developed using Flutter.  Flutter is an open-source UI toolkit enables the creation of native applications for both Android and iOS devices from a single codebase, written in Google's Dart programming language. This ensures a consistent experience across Android, iOS, and iPad devices while reducing development overhead. The Flutter apps will communicate with the backend server to sync disposal data, route assignments, and campaign status in real time. The web platform will be developed using Laravel, a PHP-based framework for building web-based administrative systems. This platform will handle account management, bin monitoring, campaign control, and analytics reporting. It will also expose a set of APIs to facilitate communication between the smart bin hardware, the mobile apps, and the backend server. The smart bin will be powered by a Raspberry Pi 5, which serves as the main processing unit for both mechanical control and on-device artificial intelligence. The bin will include a built-in camera, a touchscreen monitor, and a compartmentalized internal structure designed to separate different types of waste. Each time a user initiates a disposal session, the camera will capture an image of the waste item as it is inserted. This image will be processed directly on the device using a fine-tuned object detection model based on Ultralytics YOLOv11. The model will be trained to identify common drink-related items such as plastic lids, coated paper cups, and straws. Based on the classification result, the system will trigger the appropriate internal mechanism to direct the item into its correct compartment. The bin will also feature a range of sensors to support its automated behavior. Sensors will detect when a user approaches the bin, when an item is inserted, and when a compartment has reached its full capacity. These sensor inputs will allow the bin to respond in real time, activating scanning, sorting, and disposal steps automatically. In cases where leftover liquid is detected, the bin will be able to respond by draining the excess before sorting, reducing contamination and improving the recyclability of each item. By relying on real-time input from multiple sensing points, the bin can carry out each disposal session reliably without requiring manual intervention. Mechanical actions such as opening flaps, rotating components, and separating waste materials will be controlled through embedded software written in Python. These routines will ensure that motors and actuators respond precisely to each detection result. Higher-level processes such as image handling, AI inference, and communication with the backend system will be managed using Python. This includes syncing disposal data with the central server, updating bin status, and integrating image classification results into the broader analytics platform. At the front of the bin, a touchscreen monitor will guide users through the disposal process. The screen will display clear visual instructions, feedback messages, and progress indicators during each session. It will also confirm when a QR code has been scanned, show tips for proper recycling, and display a thank-you animation once the disposal is complete. This interactive layer ensures that the system is easy to use, accessible to first-time users, and capable of reinforcing positive behavior through instant feedback. Together, these hardware and software components will enable the smart bin to operate autonomously. It will be able to detect, classify, sort, and respond to waste disposal activities with minimal user effort, while remaining connected to the larger ecosystem for real-time coordination and tracking.  |  |  |

| **TARGET MARKET** |  |  |  |
|  |  | The primary target market for the proposed smart bin ecosystem is beverage outlets that rely heavily on single-use disposable packaging. These include a wide range of drink-focused franchises such as bubble tea stores, coffee chains, and juice bars. These industries have experienced rapid growth in Malaysia and across the globe over the past decade ([Brand 360 Degree, 2024](#brand 360 degree.-\(2024,-december 19\).-the-rise-of-tea-in-malaysia:-a-brewing-threat-to-the-coffee-market.-brand360.-https://www.brand360.com.my/case-study/the-rise-of-tea-chains-in-malaysia/); [Matcha Eight, 2025](#matcha-eight.-\(2025,-may 23\).-the-rising-popularity-of-japanese-tea-culture-in-malaysia.-matcha-eight.-https://matchaeight.com/blogs/news/the-rising-popularity-of-japanese-tea-culture-in-malaysia)). Among the most recognizable brands are Tealive, Starbucks, Chagee, Mixue, Gong Cha, Zus Coffee, Coffee Bean, Boost, Bingxue, Wedrink, Ang Ang’s Roastery, and OFFFFIVE COFFEE. What these outlets share in common is a high volume of daily transactions that generate significant amounts of waste in the form of plastic cups, coated paper cups, straws, lids, and cardboard sleeves. The operational structure of these businesses tends to prioritize speed and convenience, which leads to the extensive use of disposable packaging materials that are almost never reclaimed, sorted, or recycled effectively at the point of disposal. These outlets operate in high-footfall areas such as shopping malls, strip malls, university campuses, and urban commercial zones. Their customer base ranges from students and office workers to tourists and everyday consumers. The popularity of grab-and-go beverages contributes to a large waste footprint that is often invisible to the end consumer but highly concentrated at the outlet level. Data from an on-site interview conducted at CHAGEE, a popular tea franchise located at Karpal Singh Drive, Penang, reveals that the outlet sells between 330–380 drinks per day on weekdays, up to 700 on Fridays, and as many as 1000 on weekends and public holidays. This volume translates directly into hundreds of single-use items entering the waste stream every day, with no efforts to separate recyclable materials from non-recyclable materials and no visual cues or signage to guide customers toward sustainable behavior. Given the standardization of operations across such franchises, this pattern is likely replicated across hundreds of outlets throughout Malaysia, resulting in a cumulative waste problem that remains largely unaddressed at the source. In this context, beverage outlets are not only generators of waste but also key leverage points for sustainability intervention. Their fixed locations, standardized product lines, and consistent daily customer flow make them ideal candidates for the deployment of smart bin systems and associated digital infrastructure. More importantly, these outlets have business incentives to adopt sustainable solutions. With growing consumer awareness around environmental issues, companies are increasingly expected to demonstrate social responsibility and transparency in their operations. Studies show that Gen Z users, who make up a majority of current university populations, are significantly influenced by environmental messaging through education and social media ([Agrawal et al., 2023](#agrawal,-m.,-kalia,-p.,-nema,-p.,-zia,-a.,-kaur,-k.,-&-john,-h.-b.-\(2023\).-evaluating-the-influence-of-government-initiatives-and-social-platforms-on-green-practices-of-gen-z:-the-mediating-role-of-environmental-awareness-and-consciousness.-cleaner-and-responsible-consumption,-8,-100109.)). In addition, a survey of university students in the Klang Valley revealed that students were more likely to engage in recycling behaviour when systems were perceived as convenient and easy to understand ([Sudin et al., 2023](#sudin,-m.-a.-f.,-ma’rof,-a.-a.,-abdullah,-h.,-&-zarimohzzabeih,-z.-\(2023\).-inconvenience-and-perceived-behavioral-control-as-the-main-predictive-factors-of-recycling-behavior:-malaysian-university-students-context.-international-journal-of-academic-research-in-business-and-social-sciences,-13\(14\),-16–33.)). This value proposition is not limited to Malaysia. In fact, the need for beverage outlet-focused sustainability solutions exists globally. In countries like Japan, where the culture of recycling is deeply embedded in society, public participation in waste segregation is exceptionally high. Japanese municipalities implement strict disposal schedules and enforce meticulous sorting rules starting from early childhood education ([Luk, 2021](#luk,-k.-\(2021,-september-13\).-how-strict-waste-management-in-japan-alleviated-its-environmental-impact.-earth.org.-https://earth.org/japan-waste-management/)). Environmental practices are introduced during the formative years and reinforced through structured routines in schools. Students are taught to clean their classrooms, serve meals, and manage waste responsibly, including rinsing, flattening, and sorting milk cartons for recycling ([Web Japan, 2021](#web-japan.-\(2021\).-special-feature-on-schools-in-japan:-classroom-duties.-kids-web-japan.-https://web-japan.org/kidsweb/cool/21/202101_on-duty-activity_en.html)). Although public trash bins are scarce, citizens routinely carry their waste home and adhere to complex sorting systems, demonstrating a high level of environmental discipline ([Marcus, 2025](#marcus,-l.-\(2025,-june-3\).-japan-is-a-model-of-cleanliness.-so-where-are-all-the-trash-cans?-cnn.-https://edition.cnn.com/travel/japan-trash-cans-explainer-intl-hnk)). This social infrastructure fosters a population that is not only willing but highly competent at recycling. Meanwhile, Germany offers insight into how external incentives can shape recycling habits. The country employs a bottle deposit system known as Pfand, a German word meaning “deposit”, which adds a small fee to plastic and glass beverage containers at the point of sale ([El-Saghir, 2025](#el-saghir,-j.-\(2025,-january-20\).-pfand-in-germany-–-a-guide-to-the-bottle-return-system.-how-to-germany.com.-https://www.how-to-germany.com/pfand-in-germany/)). When consumers return the empties to dedicated machines in supermarkets, the deposit is refunded. This straightforward system transforms recycling into a behavioural loop that is reinforced by familiarity, accessibility, and financial return ([Bekkar, 2024](#bekkar,-a.-\(2024\).-the-reverse-logistics:-factors-influencing-liège-citizen's-participation-in-the-deposit-refund-scheme.)). These examples demonstrate the potential of targeted, well-designed interventions at the consumer level to change behavior and close the recycling loop. In contrast, Malaysia’s waste management culture remains underdeveloped, especially in the commercial food and beverage sector. Malaysia’s recycling rate stood at only 24.6% in 2018, despite over two decades of governmental initiatives ([Tiew et al., 2019](#tiew,-k.,-basri,-n.-e.-a.,-deng,-h.,-watanabe,-k.,-zain,-m.-f.-m.,-&-wang,-s.-\(2019\).-comparative-study-on-recycling-behaviours-between-regular-recyclers-and-non-regular-recyclers-in-malaysia.-journal-of-environmental-management,-237,-255.-https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2019.02.033)). The Waste Segregation at Source (WSAS) policy, introduced by the Penang State Government in 2016, encourages households, offices, and F\&B outlets to separate recyclables from general waste ([Penang Green Council, 2024](#penang-green-council.-\(2024\).-waste-segregation-at-source-\(wsas\).-penang-green-council.-retrieved-july-18,-2025,-from-https://www.pgc.com.my/2020/waste-segregation-at-source-wsas/)). However, the policy is not widely practiced in retail settings. Beverage outlets are major contributors to the packaging waste stream. But in reality, they receive little attention in recycling efforts. Most existing programs focus on households or public institutions. As a result, the commercial drink sector remains a critical blind spot in Malaysia’s broader sustainability strategy. These outlets already operate with centralized supply chains, brand-level marketing systems, and digital ordering platforms. These elements make the integration of additional technologies smoother and more scalable. For instance, adding user tracking to existing loyalty programs or integrating waste analytics into franchise analytical monitoring systems becomes both technically feasible and strategically appealing. Because beverage outlets are densely clustered in commercial zones, they also offer logistical advantages for features such as AI-based pickup route optimization. Even if certain stores like CHAGEE experience high-volume sales that result in daily bin saturation, other outlets in the same zone may exhibit slower fill rates. This allows for the clustering of pickup tasks to reduce travel time, fuel consumption, and labor costs. In addition, these retailers can play an active role in the system’s gamification layer. Since they are already integrated into the ecosystem, they can directly offer digital rewards such as discount vouchers, free drinks, or exclusive items through the recycling app. This removes the need to seek out third-party sponsors and strengthens the connection between the customer and the brand. By linking sustainable behavior with tangible store benefits, beverage outlets can deepen customer loyalty while contributing to environmental goals. Beverage outlets represent a uniquely attractive target market due to their potential for customer-focused innovation and brand-led engagement. These businesses are well positioned to benefit from subscription-based participation, where added features can enhance both customer interaction and brand visibility. For example, subscribing outlets could activate exclusive campaigns such as branch-specific point multipliers or limited-time digital rewards. These promotions can help drive traffic to new or underperforming locations. This approach aligns with the marketing strategies already common in beverage retail, particularly among franchises aiming to strengthen their local footprint. Beyond promotional opportunities, beverage outlets can also benefit from insights that reflect actual customer experiences. Metrics such as bin fill rate and drink residue levels can reveal patterns in consumption, customer satisfaction, and product performance. A high volume of unfinished drinks may signal a mismatch in flavor or pricing, while consistently empty cups may indicate strong acceptance. Most beverage outlets do not currently have access to such behavioral signals. This makes them especially receptive to tools that provide operational awareness while reinforcing their sustainability goals. In addition to beverage outlets, the proposed system also identifies waste collection agencies and independent recyclers as important target groups. These collectors often operate in a fragmented and informal manner, as seen at CHAGEE and other similar outlets. Despite playing a key role in the recycling chain, they typically lack access to digital tools or coordination mechanisms. This group represents a valuable but underserved segment that stands to benefit from better structure, recognition, and integration into formal sustainability efforts. These connections also support wider goals such as traceability and ESG reporting, which emphasizes the commercial and environmental value of including collectors within the broader ecosystem. |  |

| **COMPETITION/CONTRIBUTION** |  |  |  |
|  | The main competitors to the Mobius Smart Waste Bin Ecosystem include Ameru, Bin.e, the AI Audio Classifier Recycle Bin, and the Avarai Smart Bin. Each introduces a different method of automation, but none focus on personalized user tracking, mobile integration, or gamified sustainability. Another notable competitor is Klean, a Malaysian-developed container deposit system that rewards users for recycling plastic bottles and cans through its reverse vending machines ([KLEAN, n.d.](#klean.-\(n.d.\).-klean-the-world:-home.-https://www.klean.asia/)). Users accumulate points and redeem them for rewards such as Touch 'n Go eWallet credit, iQIYI subscriptions, and vouchers from Village Grocer or Lotus's ([SWCorp, 2022](#solid-waste-and-public-cleansing-management-corporation.-\(2022\).-annual-report-2022.-swcorp-malaysia.-https://www.swcorp.gov.my/en/annual-reports-2022/)). While Klean successfully promotes recycling through rewards and mobile integration, its scope remains limited to specific item types like bottles and cans, and is primarily designed for general public settings. In contrast, Mobius Smart Waste Bin Ecosystem offers a more targeted and intelligent solution tailored for beverage-focused businesses. It functions as a modular ecosystem that not only uses AI to sort complex drink-related waste like paper cups, straws, and lids, but also enables tracking of user behavior, gamified engagement, and vendor-specific waste analytics. Unlike off-the-shelf systems, Mobius Smart Waste Bin Ecosystem can be adapted to suit the branding, reporting, and operational needs of each beverage outlet, offering a more strategic and integrated approach to sustainability. Ameru is designed for office and commercial environments ([Ameru, n.d.](#ameru.-\(n.d.\).-ameru-–-revolutionizing-waste-management-with-ai.-https://www.ameru.ai/)). It uses an Nvidia Jetson development board paired with a full HD camera to identify waste types with high visual accuracy. Sorting feedback is displayed on a 10-inch touchscreen, and overall bin data is collected. However, there is no user identification, app integration, or behavioral feedback. Bin.e improves on basic sorting by including an internal compactor for plastic and paper, along with a large display for educational content ([Greenwalt, 2020](#greenwalt,-m.-\(2020,-june-2\).-bin-e-creates-contactless-smart-bin.-waste360.-https://www.waste360.com/waste-recycling/bin-e-creates-contactless-smart-bin)). It offers web-based analytics for usage monitoring ([Bin-e, n.d.](#bin-e.-\(n.d.\).-bin-e-|-smart-waste-bin.-https://www.bine.world/)). Like Ameru, it is intended for general workplace use and does not track individual users or promote sustainable habits through engagement. The AI Audio Classifier Recycle Bin is an experimental project by Samuel Alexander, submitted as a contest entry for the Hackaday Prize 2023 and published on Hackaday.io ([Alexander, 2023](#alexander,-s.-\(2023,-july-4\).-ai-audio-classifier-recycle-bin-[project].-hackaday.io.-retrieved-july-12,-2025,-from-https://hackaday.io/project/191802-ai-audio-classifier-recycle-bin)). It explores the use of edge AI audio models to classify waste based on the sound produced during disposal. This sound-based recognition approach offers a creative alternative to traditional vision systems. With the board able to identify material types by sound, the bin uses a stepper motor to rotate between four compartments and a servo-actuated trapdoor to direct the waste, all powered by a LiPo battery for wireless operation ([Arduino Team, 2023](#arduino-team.-\(2023,-july-5\).-this-recycling-bin-sorts-waste-using-audio-classification.-arduino-blog.-https://blog.arduino.cc/2023/07/05/this-recycling-bin-sorts-waste-using-audio-classification/)). However, the system remains a prototype and is limited in classification accuracy. It does not currently offer user feedback, individual tracking, or mobile integration. While the AI Audio Classifier offers an experimental spin on material detection, its audio-based classification is less reliable in noisy environments and lacks practical deployment. Mobius Smart Waste Bin Ecosystem adopts a computer vision approach. The Avarai Smart Bin, developed by Malaysia-based Avarai Technologies Sdn. Mobius contributes to the sustainability ecosystem by reframing recycling as a brand-driven, user-centric, and data-rich experience tailored to the beverage retail environment. While most waste management technologies focus on generic sorting or infrastructure-scale automation, Mobius introduces a modular and behavior-aware approach that aligns closely with the everyday operations of drink-focused outlets. Its ability to link waste disposal to individual user profiles transforms the act of throwing away a cup or straw into a traceable, personalized moment that can be reinforced with incentives, feedback, and habit-forming engagement loops. One of Mobius’ key contributions lies in its role as a flexible engagement tool for businesses. Rather than treating recycling as a compliance task, the system enables beverage outlets to become active participants in sustainability campaigns through tailored interactions. Businesses can promote seasonal challenges, increase foot traffic using limited-time reward multipliers, and associate their brand identity with exclusive in-app collectibles or achievements. These tools allow sustainability efforts to be embedded directly into the customer journey, creating value for both the business and the end user. Instead of relying on posters, verbal reminders, or unverified bin usage, each disposal is automatically logged, verified, and classified at the point of entry. This enables outlet owners and franchise operators to track customer participation, identify recurring contamination patterns, and compare waste-related performance across multiple branches. By turning recycling data into a usable resource, Mobius helps store operators align with environmental guidelines while gaining insights into user behavior and operational trends. In doing so, Mobius also directly supports broader sustainability goals, such as Sustainable Development Goal 12 on responsible consumption and SDG 11.3, which calls for sustainable, data-informed urban planning ([United Nations, 2015](#united-nations.-\(2015\).-goal-11:-make-cities-and-human-settlements-inclusive,-safe,-resilient-and-sustainable.-united-nations-department-of-economic-and-social-affairs.-https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal11)). This turns each disposal into a small behavioral loop that encourages repeat engagement. The mobile app will also include features to allow users to plant virtual trees that grow over time as they dispose responsibly, mirroring the Shopee Farm model, where users nurture virtual plants to earn real-world vouchers ([Tan, 2020](#tan,-z.-\(2020,-june-26\).-games-people-play:-how-shopee-uses-data-science-to-make-shopping-a-holistic-experience.-tech-in-asia.-https://www.techinasia.com/games-people-play-shopee-data-science-shopping-holistic-experience)). The Mobius Smart Waste Bin System aims to advance the goals of sustainable urban development and responsible consumption through an innovative blend of AI, gamification, and personalized feedback. Unlike commercially mature systems that prioritize industrial efficiency and infrastructure optimization, Mobius introduces a more creative, user-centered approach by linking each act of disposal to user identity, behavioral insight, and habit reinforcement. These are the types of mixed material waste often produced by beverage outlets. Once detected, the items are automatically sorted into the correct compartments using motorized mechanisms. This reduces contamination, keeps recyclable streams clean, and improves the chances of materials being accepted for recycling. |  | The development of the Smart Waste Bin Ecosystem follows the evolutionary prototyping model, which emphasizes building a working system early and continuously refining it through multiple iterations. This model suits projects where user feedback and real-world testing play a critical role in shaping the final outcome. Rather than aiming for a complete system from the start, this approach allows for gradual improvement, with each version becoming more functional and aligned with actual user needs. Given that Smart Waste Bin Ecosystem involves hardware integration, on-device AI, user interaction and mobile apps, and backend analytics, it is important to test each component in stages. The system will be developed through multiple phases (P1 to P6), with each phase delivering a more complete version of the overall ecosystem. At the end of each milestone, functional testing and supervised evaluation will inform what changes or additions are needed for the next version. This cycle allows the project to adapt over time while maintaining steady progress toward a fully operational system.
